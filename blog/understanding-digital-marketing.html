Understanding Digital Marketing

November 15, 2024
Marketing

What Is Digital Marketing?

Selling and promoting goods and services today runs through websites, apps, mobile devices, social media, search engines, and other digital channels — that is what digital marketing is.
In this article we explore just how far-reaching digital marketing has become, and how to put its real power to work.

Brands tap into websites, mobile apps, social media, and search engines to market and sell efficiently. Beyond classic advertising, this gives companies a fresh route to connect with buyers and study how they behave. Digital marketing took off as the internet spread in the 1990s. These days, most businesses blend online and offline tactics to reach people — while wrestling with new challenges such as channels that evolve at lightning speed and the pressure to make sense of data.

Key Takeaways

  • Digital marketing puts websites, mobile devices, social media, and search engines to work promoting goods and services — a complement to traditional marketing, not just a copy of it.
  • Online channels have grown steadily since the 1990s, and platforms such as email, social media, and pay-per-click ads each need careful planning to land with the right audience.
  • Core tactics span website marketing, content creation, email campaigns, social media, affiliate marketing, plus rising formats like video and text-message marketing.
  • Marketers must cope with channels multiplying fast, with turning mountains of data into action, and with winning attention amid constant digital noise.
  • Strong digital marketers combine clear communication, sharp data skills, and a feel for social media so they can shape stories that persuade.

Understanding the Mechanics of Digital Marketing

Selling more means picking the right mix of techniques and channels. Doing it well takes both advertising instincts and sales know-how. That work is handled either by in-house teams at individual businesses or by outside agencies serving many clients at once.

Print, television, and radio used to be the only game in town, so every marketing budget flowed there. Those outlets still matter, but the internet opened a brand-new route to customers — and digital marketing was born.

Fresh technology and shifting habits forced brands to rethink where their budgets go. Email led the first wave of online outreach. Attention then moved to search engines such as Netscape, where tagging and keywords helped businesses stand out. Later, social networks like Facebook let companies study audience data and aim messages at remarkably narrow groups.

With smartphones and tablets in nearly every pocket, brands can pitch products to shoppers anywhere, anytime. A 2022 Pew Research Center study reported that 76% of American adults had already bought something online using a phone.

Exploring Various Digital Marketing Channels

Online channels have been multiplying since the 1990s and show no sign of stopping. Here are eight of the most widely used ones today.

Website Marketing

A company’s own site usually sits at the center of its online marketing. The best ones present the brand and what it sells in a way people instantly grasp and remember. Any modern website also needs to load quickly, work well on phones, and be effortless to move through.

Pay-Per-Click Advertising

With pay-per-click (PPC) ads, brands can put paid messages in front of readers on news sites and across digital platforms. Teams can launch PPC campaigns through Google, Bing, LinkedIn, X (formerly Twitter), Pinterest, and Facebook, targeting people who search for terms tied to what they sell.

Those ads can be aimed by demographics (for instance age or gender), by interests, or by location. Google Ads and Facebook Ads remain the go-to platforms for PPC.

Content Marketing

The point of content marketing is to win over future customers with articles, images, or videos they genuinely enjoy. Brands usually publish that material on their own site, then spread it via social media, email, SEO, or even paid campaigns. It plays softer than straight advertising — the sponsor’s offering may sit quietly in the background rather than taking center stage.

Email Marketing

Email remains one of the most powerful online channels, even though plenty of inboxes treat it as spam. Most teams gather addresses through their other channels first, then use email to nudge those subscribers toward a purchase.

Social Media Marketing

Social campaigns first aim to get a brand noticed and trusted. Once that base is built, the same channels can pull in leads and even sell directly. Boosted posts and sponsored tweets both fall under this umbrella.

Affiliate Marketing

Affiliate deals rank among the oldest marketing models, and the internet gave them a second wind. Here, businesses and “influencers” recommend someone else’s products and earn a cut whenever a sale closes or a new lead signs up. Big names — Amazon included — run programs paying affiliates millions for driving sales.

Video Marketing

Shoppers constantly head to YouTube to compare options, pick up a how-to, check a review, or simply unwind. Brands can meet them there — or on Facebook Video, Instagram, and TikTok — with a dedicated video push. Video pays off best when it is woven into SEO, content, and wider social efforts.

Text Messaging

Text messages (SMS) let brands announce launches and deals straight to the pocket. Charities and political campaigns lean on texting too, both to rally supporters and to collect donations. Plenty of modern campaigns even let people pay or give with one reply.

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) in Digital Marketing

Just like their traditional counterparts, digital marketers lean on key performance indicators (KPIs). These numbers track how campaigns perform over time and stack up against the competition.

Below are a few of the KPIs teams watch most closely to judge whether their work is landing:

  • Click-through rate: this shows how well an online ad pulls its weight, by dividing the people who clicked it by everyone who could have seen it.
  • Conversion rate:  this goes a step beyond clicks, comparing the share of people who took the desired step — a purchase, say — against the full audience the ad or promotion reached.
  • Social media traffic: this counts how people engage with a brand’s social profiles: likes, follows, views, shares, and any other trackable action.
  • Website traffic: this counts visits to a brand’s site over a set period. It doubles as a rough gauge of whether marketing is actually sending shoppers through the door.

Digital Marketing Challenges

The online landscape throws a few curveballs at marketers. New channels keep appearing, and teams have to learn each one and squeeze value from it. They also sit on far more data than they can easily digest, and turning that flood into decisions is its own skill.

On top of that, shoppers swim in ads and alerts all day, so cutting through the clutter to earn a moment of attention keeps getting harder.

What Is a Digital Marketing Agency?

A digital marketing agency lives and breathes online outreach for other businesses. It plans and runs client campaigns across social feeds, paid search, video, and bespoke websites — among other avenues.

What Is SEO in Digital Marketing?

Search engine optimization (SEO) is the toolkit brands use to pull more visitors to their site and climb higher in results. The closer to the top a page sits, the more often people notice it — and the more clicks it tends to win.

What Is Internet Marketing?

Strictly speaking, internet marketing happens only on the internet — which makes it a slice of the bigger digital-marketing pie. Digital marketing reaches further, using every kind of digital platform, internet included, to find its audience.

How Can You Become a Digital Marketer?

Strong writing and social fluency come first, backed by a solid grip on data analytics. Most roles ask for a bachelor's degree, and courses or a digital “boot camp” can sharpen the edge further. An internship during study never hurts either.2 A master's in the field can help but is rarely a must.

What Skills Are Needed in Digital Marketing?

Clear, confident communication sits at the heart of telling a brand’s story. Analytics know-how matters just as much for reading campaign results and spotting where to improve — and social media fluency rounds out the essentials.

What Is Implicit Bias in Digital Marketing?

Implicit — or unconscious — bias slips negative stereotypes about groups into messages without anyone meaning it. Advertising and marketing have been grappling with it lately. Online, it can show up in something as small as the stock photos behind a campaign: brands defaulting to heterosexual White faces, for example, while leaving out Black, Indigenous, and other people of color, as well as different body types and abilities. Campaigns may even lean on “neutral” algorithms — yet those algorithms are built by people who can carry the same hidden biases into them.

The Bottom Line

How people consume media in the 21st century has pushed marketers to bet big — sometimes exclusively — on digital channels. Online promotion borrows plenty from print, TV, and radio, yet it brings its own toolkit too, and winning marketers make sure they master it.
